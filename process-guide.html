State COB Guidelines — Individual Plans

From the Individual Plans State COB Guidelines – CB QRG (rev. 02/10/2026) — coordinating benefits between Anthem-branded individual plans and group coverage, state by state.

  • Use alongside the Individual Plan Guidelines – CB when coordinating benefits with Elevance Health/Anthem individual plans.
  • Court orders supersede state laws when determining primacy.
  • COB prohibited states: pay the individual plan as if there is no other coverage.
  • No state regs: call to question the COB clause in the EOC before applying NAIC guidelines.
  • Exception: state mandates do not extend to self-funded ASO accounts unless exempt from ERISA (government/religious groups) — consult the EOC for ASO ERISA cases.

Overage Dependent (OAD) Primacy Rules

From the OAD Primacy Rules – CB QRG (rev. 04/23/2026) — which plan pays first when an overage dependent has multiple coverages.

  • Order of precedence: court order → active/inactive → birthday rule / longer-shorter rule, depending on the scenario.
  • Do not flip an existing primary record to secondary for the entire span — it impacts claims.
  • When a COB letter is required, refer to “Determine Which Letter to Send a Dependent – CB”.
  • Aetna OADs use their own hierarchy — see the Aetna section below.
  • Age of emancipation varies by state — check the NCSL Age of Majority site.

Dependent & OAD Letters — FUSE

From “Determine Which Letter to Send a Dependent – CB” (rev. 08/04/2026) — which COB letter to send, and when.

  • Letters are sent when additional information is needed to determine primacy.
  • Age of majority varies by state — check the NCSL Age of Majority list for the dependent's state of residence.
  • As of 06/30/2025: no court decree + determinable parental relationship → use the birthday rule (or NAIC COB rules), send a divorce letter, and close the case.
  • Divorce letter only when: relationship is unclear, the member mentioned a court decree, or OHI details are needed and resources are exhausted.
  • 24 months = recovery lookback period, reviewed case-by-case — ask your OE (Operations Expert) when unclear.

Quick Glossary

Terms you’ll see in the queue every day.

  • COBCoordination of Benefits — deciding which plan pays first when a member has more than one.
  • eCOBElectronic Coordination of Benefits — COB handled through automated data exchanges and work queues.
  • PrimacyWhich payer is primary (pays first) vs. secondary for a given claim.
  • TPLThird-Party Liability — another party (often another insurer) that should pay before we do.
  • SMESubject-Matter Expert — your first escalation for tricky cases.
  • Watch zoneA metric within 2 points of its goal — on track, but keep an eye on it.
  • NAICNational Association of Insurance Commissioners — issues the five-character insurance codes used in INS CD.
  • INS CDInsurance Code field on the COB Limited Liability record in WGS — always a 5-char NAIC code.
  • COB LLCOB Limited Liability — the WGS record type that carries the other-coverage insurance code.
  • BOBBlue on Blue — the member is covered by two Blue plans; uses system-specific INS CD values, not the NAIC list.
  • QCRFallback INS CD when an insurer isn't on the NAIC list — enter QCR and type the company name.
  • CS90 KernelLegacy group platform — its records always take INS CD U07521 instead of QCR.
  • ASOAdministrative Services Only — a self-funded employer account; state COB mandates usually don't apply unless the group is ERISA-exempt.
  • ERISAEmployee Retirement Income Security Act — federal law for group plans; exempt groups (government, religious) may still follow state mandates.
  • EOCEvidence of Coverage — the policy document that states whether a plan coordinates benefits and how primacy is set.
  • OICOther Insurance Carrier — the member's additional coverage, identified during COB investigation to determine primacy.
  • QRGQuick Reference Guide — the source documents behind this page.
  • OADOverage Dependent — a dependent past the standard age limit; special primacy rules apply.
  • Birthday ruleThe plan of the parent whose birthday falls earlier in the year is primary.
  • Longer/shorter ruleThe plan effective longer is primary — for OADs, compare the dependent's effective dates.
  • Active/inactive ruleAn active plan beats a retiree/COBRA (inactive) plan — applied before other rules except court orders.
  • COBRAFederal continuation coverage; counts as an inactive plan for primacy.
  • GovernanceTeam that resolves unclear primacy cases (e.g., EFF date equals hire date).
  • Residency/Custodial ruleAetna OAD rule — primacy follows where the dependent lives; compare the address on both carriers.
  • Aetna OAD hierarchyAetna's strict order: Court Order → Residency/Custodial → Birthday → Longer/Shorter.
  • FUSEThe system used to generate and send COB letters to dependents.
  • OHIOther Health Insurance — additional coverage info that may determine primacy.
  • OEOperations Expert — escalation contact for things like the 24-month lookback period.
  • Divorce letterSent only when the relationship is unclear, a court decree was mentioned, or resources are exhausted.
  • Commercial COB letterSent to an OAD when additional details are required to determine primacy.
  • General information letterSent to a dependent when plans match but more information is needed.
  • Lookback periodThe 24-month recovery window reviewed case-by-case for OAD primacy — ask an OE when unclear.
  • Overlap periodThe date range where Anthem coverage and the other insurance are both active — use the calculator above.
  • EFF dateEffective date — when coverage started; compared with hire date in unclear primacy cases.
  • UHCUnited Healthcare — for OADs, court order while in effect, else longer/shorter rule (UHC only, not the umbrella).
  • Kaiser PermanenteUses the birthday rule for all OAD coordination; longer/shorter only when plans share a subscriber.
